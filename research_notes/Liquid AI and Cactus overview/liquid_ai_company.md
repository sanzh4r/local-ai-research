## 1. Founders, founding date and research lineage

### Takeaway
Liquid AI was founded in March 2023 in Cambridge, Massachusetts as an MIT CSAIL spin-off by Ramin Hasani (CEO), Mathias Lechner (CTO), Alexander Amini (Chief Scientific Officer) and MIT professor Daniela Rus; it emerged from stealth in December 2023. Its lineage is the "liquid neural network" research line (Liquid Time-constant Networks, 2018-2020).

### Cited Findings
- Founding team: CEO Ramin Hasani, CTO Mathias Lechner, Chief Scientific Officer Alexander Amini, and MIT Professor Daniela Rus (four co-founders). [secondary] — [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/mit-spinoff-unveils-new-generation-of-ai-models-with-liquid-neural-network-technology/)

- One profile gives an incorporation date of 30 March 2023 and stealth exit in December 2023; describes it as a spin-off from MIT's Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory. [aggregator/unverified] — [aiwiki.ai](https://www.aiwiki.ai/wiki/liquid_ai)
- Liquid neural network research dates to initial work in 2018 and gained attention with the "Liquid Time-constant Networks" paper in 2020. [aggregator] — [StartupHub.ai](https://startuphub.ai/startups/liquid-ai)
- In Daniela Rus's MIT lab, liquid neural networks run directly on soft-bodied and ingestible robots (research context, not a commercial product). — [Wikipedia: Liquid AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_AI)
- Research arm formally named "Liquid Labs" ("our team driving fundamental breakthroughs in the science of building intelligent, personalized, and adaptive machines"; commitment that progress "should be transparent, reproducible, and shared"; continues publishing technical reports and weights). ## 3. Product lineup

### Takeaway
Liquid AI ships (a) open-weight Liquid Foundation Model families — LFM2 (Jul 2025), LFM2.5 (Jan-Oct 2026), LFM3 (27 Oct 2026) — plus task-specific "Liquid Nanos", the "d1" decision-model family and LiquidCodec; (b) a developer/deployment stack — LEAP (Liquid Edge AI Platform: SDK, model library, fine-tuning scripts, bundling CLI), the Apollo on-device chat/test app, a web Playground and a hosted API/Console; and (c) custom enterprise models under commercial licence. Weights are released under the LFM Open License v1.0, free for commercial use only below $10M annual revenue.

## 4. Business model, named customers and partners

### Takeaway
Revenue comes primarily from licensing models and software to device manufacturers and enterprises — typically an annual per-device fee plus development (customisation) fees delivered by an assigned applied-ML team — with the open-weight releases acting as a funnel via the $10M-revenue licence threshold. Named commercial relationships: Mercedes-Benz, Shopify, AMD (investor/technical partner), G42, Insilico Medicine, MacPaw, Capgemini, plus silicon/distribution launch partners.

### Cited Findings
Business model
- TheStreet (c. 20 Oct 2026, interview with Hasani): Liquid makes money primarily by licensing models and software to manufacturers, generally an annual per-device fee in addition to development costs; Hasani estimated licensing revenue from under $1 to hundreds of dollars per device depending on application; no revenue forecasts disclosed. [secondary] — [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/stocks/liquid-ai-ceo-says-ai-next-big-opportunity-inside-car-assistant)

[blog/unverified] — [secondnewsletter Substack](https://secondnewsletter.substack.com/p/the-45-billion-dollar-bet-that-intelligence)
