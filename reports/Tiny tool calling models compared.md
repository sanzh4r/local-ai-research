# Tiny Tool Callers Win Only When Fine-Tuned

No single model wins the tiny on-device tool-calling niche. The approach that wins is a system: a small generative model fine-tuned on your own tools, a grammar that makes malformed output impossible, a gate that decides whether to act at all, and a larger fallback for anything ambiguous. Within that pattern the choice of model follows from the memory budget. Cactus Needle (14 to 29 MB) is the only option that fits microcontroller-class and wearable-class hardware, and it handles direct commands over a handful of tools in roughly 100 ms on a Raspberry Pi. Google's FunctionGemma 270M and Liquid's LFM2.5-230M/350M (150 to 290 MB) are the safer choice wherever a few hundred megabytes are available: LFM2.5 is the strongest of the three without tuning on general function calling, and FunctionGemma has the best fine-tuning evidence, including the only independent head-to-head. Liquid's d1 "decision models" do not compete for this job. They choose among options in one pass and cannot compose arguments, so they serve as routers and approval gates beside a generator. The evidence is weak throughout. Every table that contains all three vendors' models was produced by Cactus, untuned exact-match accuracy sits around 60 to 70% for all of them, and the same model appears with a BFCL v4 score of 21 in one source and 61 in another. Independent hands-on tests are consistently less flattering than vendor charts, especially for Needle. This report explains the mechanics, lays out what each model is and how it performs, grades the evidence, and corrects several statements in the earlier Liquid AI and Cactus overview.

## Tool calling is a switchboard job, and three add-ons make it fit in megabytes

**Tool calling** means translating a messy sentence into a function name plus typed arguments. The app, not the model, then runs the function. An independent paper states the two decisions involved in every request: "which tool to invoke, and whether any available tool applies", that is, selection and abstention ([arXiv 2609.18672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18672)). Think of a switchboard operator. The operator hears "dim the lounge to 30", looks at the directory of extensions they were handed (the tool schemas), plugs the call into `set_lights` and writes "lounge, 30" on the slip. The operator never needs to know how lights work. Cactus builds its whole product on this framing: tool calling "is fundamentally retrieval-and-assembly (match query to tool name, extract argument values, emit JSON), not reasoning" ([Cactus, Needle blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle)), and that is "why 45M parameters suffice whereas chat requires billions" ([Cactus, Needle 2 blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-2)). The claim is half right. The independent failures described later are almost all cases where the request did need a little reasoning.

**Constrained decoding** is the first add-on. A language model writes one token at a time by scoring every token in its vocabulary. A grammar compiled from the tool schemas can cross out every token that would break the format before the model picks. The analogy is a paper form with tick boxes: the operator physically cannot write outside the boxes. Needle 3 compiles "ranges, enums, patterns, lengths and item counts" into the grammar, "so an invalid value is unrepresentable rather than merely discouraged" ([Cactus, tool design guide](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/designing-tools-for-needle)). The grammar also saves time, because the engine skips scoring tokens that are already illegal, "up to 98% of the vocabulary projection on structural tokens" ([Cactus, Needle 2 blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-2)). The limit is the important lesson: the operator can still tick the wrong box. The independent paper puts it exactly: "constraining a decoder to a tool grammar repairs malformed output without improving the choice" ([arXiv 2609.18672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18672)). Format was never the main problem anyway. In Cactus's own BFCL run, well-formed rates were 93 to 100% for all four models while accuracy ranged from 42.6 to 61.7 ([Cactus, Needle 2 blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-2)).

**Quantization** is the second add-on. A model's weights are numbers, normally stored at 16 bits each. Quantization rounds them so each takes fewer bits, like shrinking a recipe card by rounding every quantity. At 4 bits each weight is one of sixteen values; at 2 bits, one of four. Rounding a finished model that hard usually ruins it, so Cactus trains with the rounding in place from the first step ("we never quantize post-hoc"), which is like a cook who learned the recipe with only quarter-cup measures and never knew finer ones ([Cactus, Needle 2 blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-2)). Its "CQ2" format costs about 2.125 bits per weight ([Cactus, .cact format post](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/cact-format)). The arithmetic checks out: 45M weights at 2.125 bits is about 12 MB, which with the tokenizer and engine gives the 14 MB Needle 2 file. The "nothing lost" claim means no loss relative to the model's own training precision. It has not been independently replicated, and no full-precision Needle 2 exists to compare against.

**Abstention and confidence** form the third add-on. A wrong chat answer is a typo in a letter; a wrong tool call is the letter posted to the wrong address. Every vendor therefore adds a "pause and ask" layer. Needle returns an empty call when no tool fits and attaches a confidence score, with suggested bands of act at 0.7 or above, confirm in the middle, refuse otherwise ([Cactus, confidence post](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-confidence)). The independent study above found that abstention, not selection, is where a neural component is truly required, since simple keyword retrieval picked the right tool for 162 of 164 literally matching requests but could not tell in-catalogue from out-of-catalogue requests ([arXiv 2609.18672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18672)).

Two further mechanics matter. Tool definitions sit in the prompt, like reading the whole menu aloud before each order, and every token of menu costs time and memory. Needle therefore shows the model at most five tools, chosen by similarity to the request, and warns that "an unselected tool is unreachable, not merely unlikely" ([Hugging Face, needle2 card](https://huggingface.co/Cactus-Compute/needle2)). And a **single-pass decision model** takes a different route entirely: instead of writing an answer, it is shown the options and the answer is read off as probabilities in one pass. That is a multiple-choice exam marked by scanning the bubble sheet, where the other models write an essay. Liquid's d1 code says so directly: "Nothing is decoded, so a schema violation is impossible" ([d1-3B runner.py](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/d1-3B/raw/main/runner.py)).

## Four designs: a copier, a compact chat model, a fine-tuning base and a bubble-sheet reader

### Needle is three different architectures under one name

Cactus calls all three versions "Simple Attention Networks", but the design changed each time. **Needle v1** (12 May 2026) is a 26M-parameter encoder-decoder with no feed-forward layers at all, trained on synthetic examples generated by Gemini ([Hugging Face, needle card](https://huggingface.co/Cactus-Compute/needle)). "Distilled from Gemini" here means imitation of the big model's outputs; the founder stated that "the distillation process did not access the weights", and commenters objected that this breaches Gemini's terms of service ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48111896)). **Needle 2** (9 August 2026) is a 45M-parameter, 27-layer decoder-style stack that adds a cheap feed-forward substitute (a "Hadamard MLP") and hashed lookup tables called engrams, so it is no longer attention-only. It uses a 256-token sliding attention window with the tool list pinned so it cannot be forgotten, which caps RAM at about 28 MB, and it shipped as one 14 MB binary containing engine and weights ([Cactus, Needle 2 blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-2)). **Needle 3** (17 to 18 September 2026) is a 121M-parameter, 20-layer model trained as a "ladder": any depth from 2 to 20 layers is a usable model, from 25M to 121M parameters ([Cactus, Needle 3 page](https://cactuscompute.com/needle)). About 70.8M of its 121M parameters are lookup-table rows that are read, not multiplied, which is how a larger model stays cheap per token ([Cactus blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog)). It adds structured extraction and text embeddings, and it generates no free text at all ([Cactus, Python docs](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-python-docs)).

A plain summary: Needle does not know things, it copies. It matches the user's words to a tool name and copies spans of the request into argument slots. Needle 3 enforces this as a rule: "a call contains only values evidenced by the request" ([Cactus, tool design guide](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/designing-tools-for-needle)). Much of the shipped behaviour is hand-written logic around the network: developer-written regular-expression "triggers" that force a tool, and "grounding gates" that withhold calls. Cactus says the same 121M model "moved by close to twenty points on a mobile benchmark" once grounding was enforced ([Cactus, tool design guide](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/designing-tools-for-needle)). Needle 3's benchmark gains over Needle 2 therefore cannot be credited to the neural network alone. Needle 2 and 3 are Apache 2.0, including prebuilt binaries ([GitHub](https://github.com/cactus-compute/cactus)), but the best fine-tune (all ladder depths, 2-bit output, retrained confidence head) runs only on Cactus's hosted platform; the local LoRA route exports at 4 bits and reports confidence as `None` ([Cactus, fine-tuning post](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/finetuning-needle)).

### LFM2.5-230M and 350M are small chat models trained to call tools

Liquid's models are ordinary generative language models, 14 and 16 layers of mostly gated short convolutions with six attention blocks, with a 32K context ([Hugging Face, LFM2.5-230M](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-230M)). They call tools by writing text in a trained format: a Python-style call between two special tokens, for example `<|tool_call_start|>[get_candidate_status(candidate_id="12345")]<|tool_call_end|>`, followed by a plain-language answer ([Hugging Face, LFM2.5-350M](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-350M)). Nothing in the model enforces a correct call. A parser or a grammar in the runtime does that; llama.cpp ships a dedicated parser ([Liquid docs](https://docs.liquid.ai/deployment/on-device/llama-cpp/function-calling.md)). Unlike Needle, these models can chat, summarise a tool result and emit several calls per turn. They were trained on vastly more text (19 trillion tokens for the 230M model, about 120 times Needle 2's total by Cactus's count) ([Cactus, Needle 2 blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-2)). Liquid recommends them for "data extraction and lightweight on-device agentic pipelines", not for maths, code or creative writing, and says to fine-tune for best results ([Hugging Face, LFM2.5-230M](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-230M)). The licence is Apache-like but withdraws commercial rights from any legal entity with annual revenue of $10M or more, parents and affiliates included, even though Liquid's blog says "without restrictions" ([LFM Open License](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-350M/raw/main/LICENSE)). Liquid's earlier task-specific "Nanos" for tool calling and extraction are now deprecated in favour of these general models ([Liquid docs, deprecations](https://docs.liquid.ai/lfm/help/deprecations.md)).

### FunctionGemma 270M is a base that Google tells you to fine-tune

FunctionGemma (18 December 2025) is Gemma 3 270M retrained for text-only function calling. It emits calls in its own control-token format, not JSON, such as `<start_function_call>call:get_current_temperature{location:<escape>London<escape>}<end_function_call>`, and has no thinking mode by design ([FunctionGemma card, Unsloth mirror](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/functiongemma-270m-it)). Google is unusually direct about its role: it "is intended to be fine-tuned for your specific function-calling task" and is "not intended for use as a direct dialogue model" ([FunctionGemma card](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/functiongemma-270m-it)). It has the widest runtime support of the group (LiteRT-LM, Ollama, llama.cpp, MLX, ONNX), and Google's edge team was still publishing 270M artefacts in November 2026 ([Hugging Face listing](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=litert-community&search=function&limit=20)). The licence is Google's custom Gemma terms, not an open-source licence. No "FunctionGemma 2" exists eleven months on; Gemma 4 has native function calling but its smallest model has 2.3B effective parameters ([Gemma 4 E2B card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E2B-it)).

### d1 decision models choose and gate, but do not write arguments

A d1 model takes a "state" (text, images, audio) plus questions and returns probabilities "without generating any tokens" ([Liquid, d1 blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/d1-decision-model)). In the decoder-based versions there is no special classification head: each option is mapped to a single token and the answer is a softmax over those tokens at one position ([d1-3B prompt.py](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/d1-3B/raw/main/prompt.py)). Later versions can also point at a value already written in the input, but "values are read, not converted" ([Liquid docs, decision models](https://docs.liquid.ai/lfm/models/decision-models)). Liquid's own rule of thumb draws the line: "If the answer is one of N known options, or a value already written in the input, use a decision model. If the answer is a new string the model must compose, use an LLM" ([Liquid, decision model guide](https://docs.liquid.ai/guides/decision-model-guide.md)). For tool calling, d1 can therefore pick which tool, decide whether to act and copy a literal argument. It cannot turn "five minutes" into `300`. Liquid's own robotics work pairs a fine-tuned d1-2B "skill monitor" with a generative orchestrator ([Liquid, LFM3 blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm3)). Size also rules it out of the tiny tier: d1-2B is about 1.05 GB at 4-bit, and the only small one, d1-omni-600M, is labelled an early research release with no published speeds and scores 15.1 on the Decision Index "Tools" sub-score against 74.5 for d1-3B ([Hugging Face, d1-omni-600M](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/d1-omni-600M)). The newest versions, d1.2 and d1.2-ultra, are hosted API only ([Liquid, d1.2 blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/d1-2)).

### The wider field is thin below 1B, and Apple's model is a different weight class

Below 1B parameters the other real contenders are Alibaba's general models Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3.5-0.8B (Apache 2.0) and the fine-tuned specialist Hammer 2.1-0.5B (non-commercial licence) ([Qwen3-0.6B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B); [Hammer card](https://huggingface.co/MadeAgents/Hammer2.1-0.5b)). Most other names people cite (xLAM-1b, Arch-Function-1.5B, TinyAgent-1.1B, Octopus v2, SmolLM3, Phi-4-mini) are 1B to 4B, and several carry non-commercial licences. One older design is worth knowing: Octopus v2 turned each function into a single dedicated token, which cut prompt length by 95% and scored 99.5% on its own benchmark, at the price of freezing the tool set into the weights ([arXiv 2404.01744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01744)). Apple's Foundation Models framework gives apps a bundled on-device model of roughly 3B parameters with typed, constrained tool calling at zero download cost, on Apple Intelligence-capable devices only. That description rests on search summaries of Apple's documentation and third-party guides, not direct reads ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/tool.md)).

Two readings follow. First, the speed rows come from different engines and settings and are not a controlled comparison, but the gap is large enough to matter: a tool call is a few dozen tokens, so a guest tutorial on Raspberry Pi's site measured Needle 2 at **76 to 149 ms per command** on a Pi 5 ([Raspberry Pi](https://www.raspberrypi.com/news/turn-text-input-into-actions-with-needle-a-14mb-function-calling-llm/)), while an independent CPU laptop test put FunctionGemma at 435 ms per prompt, the fastest of 21 models it tried ([Local Agent Bench](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MikeVeerman/tool-calling-benchmark/master/README.md)). Second, FunctionGemma barely shrinks under quantization (292 MB at 8-bit, 253 MB at 4-bit) ([Hugging Face file tree](https://huggingface.co/api/models/unsloth/functiongemma-270m-it-GGUF/tree/main)). Cactus's explanation is that 170M of its 270M parameters are a 262k-token embedding table ([Cactus, Needle 2 blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-2)); Needle's vocabulary is 8,192 tokens.

On accuracy, the only tables covering all three vendors are Cactus's. The Needle 2 launch table (August 2026) used base checkpoints with no fine-tuning and "ordered strict exact match", in which a row passes only if names, order and every argument value match ([Cactus, Needle 2 blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-2)):

| Benchmark (rows) | LFM2.5-230M | FunctionGemma 270M | Needle 2 | Apple on-device (~3B) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile Actions (961) | **69.1** | 64.0 | 63.7 | 57.6 |
| Mobile Actions, tool name only | 93.0 | 87.3 | **98.3** | 94.2 |
| DroidCall (200) | 11.0 | **17.5** | 17.0 | n/a |
| Seal-Tools in-domain (700) | 26.9 | 16.3 | **32.6** | n/a |
| BFCL v4 single-turn (3,641) | 60.8 | 46.1 | 42.6 | **61.7** |
| BFCL irrelevance (correctly not calling) | **77.7** | 72.1 | 60.8 | 28.3 |

The table teaches three things. Needle 2 picks the right tool 98.3% of the time and still gets the full call right only 63.7% of the time, so the losses are in the arguments. LFM2.5-230M is the best general function caller of the three small models and the best at declining. And absolute reliability is low for everyone: DroidCall scores of 11 to 17% and zero for all three on two-call rows mean "trading wins" happens at a level no one would ship unattended. Cactus states the asymmetries itself: baselines ran at 16-bit precision (which favours them), and Needle is a specialist against general models (which favours Needle) ([Cactus, Needle 2 blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-2)). A third asymmetry goes unstated. Needle was measured as model plus engine (grammar, retrieval, gates), while the baselines ran as raw checkpoints.

Needle 3's launch chart (September 2026) reports the full 121M model at **86.0** on Mobile Actions, 47.0 on DroidCall and 50.2 on BFCL v4, against LFM2.5-350M at 72.8, 32.5 and 59.1 and the cloud model DeepSeek V4 Flash at 88.4, 60.5 and 77.2 ([Cactus, Needle 3 page](https://cactuscompute.com/needle)). The headline "beats models 10× its size" holds on Mobile Actions only; on BFCL Needle 3 trails LFM2.5-350M, Qwen3.5-0.8B and LFM2.5-1.2B. The small ladder slices are weak untuned: the 8 MB, 4-layer model scores 11.7 on Mobile Actions, so the "8 MB" headline is useful only after fine-tuning ([Cactus, Needle 3 page](https://cactuscompute.com/needle)).

Fine-tuning changes every number. Google reports FunctionGemma moving from 58% to 85% on Mobile Actions ([FunctionGemma card](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/functiongemma-270m-it)), and an independent paper re-measured Google's tuned specialist at 90.3% ([arXiv 2609.25373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25373)). The partner distil labs reports LFM2.5-350M going from 61.4% to 98.0% on a shell-command task and FunctionGemma from 38.8% to 96.7% on a six-tool smart-home task ([distil labs, LFM2.5](https://huggingface.co/distil-labs/distil-lfm25-shellper); [distil labs, FunctionGemma](https://huggingface.co/distil-labs/distil-home-assistant-functiongemma)). Cactus's chart shows Needle 2 going from 17.0 to 74.5 on DroidCall when tuned ([Cactus, Needle 2 blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-2)). The only independent comparison in which one person fine-tuned rival models on one task went heavily against Needle: on 230 test cases for a game-database tool interface, tuned FunctionGemma got exact arguments right in **85.2%** of cases, tuned Needle 3 in 20.4% and tuned Needle 2 in 18.7% ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49748553)). That is one anonymous user with a private dataset in a domain outside Needle's training distribution. It is also the only data point of its kind.

For d1, no head-to-head against any generative tool caller exists. Latency is attractive where hardware allows: 5.7 ms per question on an H100 and 18.6 ms on an Apple M5 Pro for d1-2B ([Hugging Face, d1-2B](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/d1-2B)). The smallest hardware with a published figure is a Jetson Orin Nano at 50 ms for d1-3B ([Liquid, Open d1](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/d1-open)). No phone or CPU-only numbers exist as text.

## The numbers conflict, and almost none of them are independent

**Who measured what.** Every Needle accuracy figure is Cactus's own; no independent run on a public benchmark was found. Liquid's tables compare against Granite, Qwen3.5-0.8B and Gemma 3 1B and never mention Needle or FunctionGemma ([Hugging Face, LFM2.5-230M](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-230M)). Google publishes only BFCL sub-scores and its own Mobile Actions pair. The independent evidence consists of one 12-prompt, 3-tool benchmark, one arXiv paper on FunctionGemma, and user reports on Hacker News. The Raspberry Pi article is a guest post by Cactus ("our friends from Cactus Compute show how"), so its measurements are not arm's-length even though Raspberry Pi hosts them ([Raspberry Pi](https://www.raspberrypi.com/news/turn-text-input-into-actions-with-needle-a-14mb-function-calling-llm/)).

**The BFCL v4 conflict.** Liquid reports LFM2.5-230M at **21.03** and LFM2.5-350M at **21.86** on "BFCLv4" ([Hugging Face, LFM2.5-230M](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-230M)). Cactus reports LFM2.5-230M at **60.8** on "BFCL v4 single-turn" in August and at **46.3** on "BFCL v4" in September, with LFM2.5-350M at **59.1** ([Cactus, Needle 2 blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-2); [Cactus, Needle 3 page](https://cactuscompute.com/needle)). The most likely explanation for Liquid versus Cactus is scope. BFCL v4 adds web-search and memory tasks that tiny models cannot attempt, and Cactus scored only the 13 single-turn categories with an unweighted mean. Neither vendor states this, so the reconciliation is an inference. The 14-point drop for LFM2.5-230M between Cactus's own two charts, while FunctionGemma barely moved (46.1 to 46.6), is unexplained. The practical rule: a "BFCL score" without version, subset and harness is close to meaningless. A model card that lists SmolLM3 at 92.3 on "BFCL" and a table that puts GPT-4o at 62% overall on BFCL v3 describe different tests ([SmolLM3 card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM3-3B); [Arch-Function card](https://huggingface.co/katanemo/Arch-Function-1.5B)).

**Mobile Actions: untuned versus tuned.** Mobile Actions is Google's dataset, built to train FunctionGemma on Android system tools ([dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/mobile-actions)). Four figures circulate for FunctionGemma on it: 58% untuned (Google), 64.0 and 65.1 untuned (Cactus, two charts, strict ordered match), 85% tuned (Google) and 90.3% for Google's tuned model as re-measured independently. Cactus's charts place untuned Needle 3 (86.0) beside untuned FunctionGemma (65.1). The like-for-like comparison is tuned against tuned, where FunctionGemma reaches 85 to 90, Needle 2 reaches 84.6 and Needle 3's full model reaches 84.5 by Cactus's own figures ([Cactus, Needle 3 page](https://cactuscompute.com/needle)). On that basis they are level, and Needle achieves it at about one tenth of the size. Two caveats attach. Cactus does not say whether Mobile Actions-like data entered Needle 3's training corpus; it says only that base models were measured on benchmarks they "never saw". And Mobile Actions is an in-distribution test: the independent paper found Google's tuned specialist falls to **17.2%** on fifteen neighbouring device-control categories, below the untuned base at 29.3% ([arXiv 2609.25373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25373)). Fine-tuning buys accuracy on the trained tools and costs generality.

**Selective headlines.** "Can match DeepSeek V4 Flash" is true for tuned Needle 3 slices on DroidCall, a 200-row benchmark, after tuning on that benchmark's training data (62.5 to 70 against 60.5). On Mobile Actions no tuned slice passes DeepSeek, and tuning the full model slightly lowered its score (86.7 to 84.5) ([Cactus, Needle 3 page](https://cactuscompute.com/needle)). Cactus's charts also mix two scoring modes ("confidence gate on" and "forced calls") and its baseline numbers drift between posts, so the last digit is noise. Cactus has published no fine-tuned-baseline comparison and no false-accept curves for its confidence score.

**Other vendors' soft spots.** Liquid's 2025 Nano tool-calling results were on a private benchmark shown only as an image. Its d1 numbers are all self-run, and two differently named leaderboards appear in its posts (a "Decision Index" hosted as a third-party Hugging Face Space and an "Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1") whose relationship was not established ([Hugging Face, d1-3B](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/d1-3B); [Liquid, d1.2 blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/d1-2)). Liquid's own multi-turn scores are low: LFM2.5-230M scores 5.26 on τ²-Bench Telecom ([Hugging Face, LFM2.5-230M](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-230M)). The independent Local Agent Bench is 12 prompts and 3 tools, and its author showed that 3-run results were a small-sample artefact ([Local Agent Bench](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MikeVeerman/tool-calling-benchmark/master/README.md)).

## Every small model fails at the same place: when the request needs judgement

The failures are consistent across vendors and are the best guide to what these models are. The independent Local Agent Bench concluded that "the hard problem isn't generating well-formed JSON. It's deciding whether to act at all". FunctionGemma was fastest with perfect restraint but fell into keyword traps, scoring 0.640 against 0.880 for the general Qwen3-0.6B, which took 3,410 ms to FunctionGemma's 435 ms ([Local Agent Bench](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MikeVeerman/tool-calling-benchmark/master/README.md)). That result captures the trade in this niche: tiny specialists buy speed and footprint by giving up the reasoning that ambiguous requests need.

Needle's public demos show the pattern most vividly because it reasons least. Users reported "Make it a little warmer in here" setting the thermostat to cool, "5° warmer" setting the temperature to 5°F, and "turn on the tv" locking a door named "tv" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49246804)). With Needle 3, "25 minute timer" became `duration_seconds: 25` "with 100% confidence", "wake me up at 6 30" became a 630-second timer, and "My car crashed I need help" played music at confidence 1 ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49748553)). These are exactly what a copier does: it copies the number and cannot convert it. They also undercut the confidence safety net, because a fluent copy error looks confident. One tester found the model "never declined anything with our full tool set", and Cactus's own table shows Needle 2 wrongly calling a tool on about four in ten irrelevant BFCL prompts. Cactus staff concede the limits: the model "can struggle with implied references and multistep reasoning... for some things you just have to have a model that is properly sized" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49748553)). Non-English use is weaker again: Cactus's docs note that "correct Spanish calls have been measured at 0.0" confidence ([Cactus, confidence post](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-confidence)). The fair summary is that Needle is robust to rewording of direct commands and not to implication, relative values or unit conversion. Critics on Hacker News asked whether regular expressions plus a classifier would do as well; Cactus has not published that baseline.

FunctionGemma and LFM2.5 fail more quietly but in the same directions. FunctionGemma's own card shows parallel calls at 39 and "live parallel multiple" at 20.8, and irrelevance at 73.7, so roughly one irrelevant query in four still triggers a call ([FunctionGemma card](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/functiongemma-270m-it)). Untuned, both it and LFM2.5-350M sit near 40 to 63% on narrow multi-turn tasks before tuning lifts them above 95%. A launch-day sceptic's objection still stands: "I would be wary of having a LLM with 85% accuracy call tools on my system" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46316533)). The d1 models avoid format and fluency errors by construction, but Liquid itself says to "calibrate any decision threshold on data from your own use" ([Hugging Face, d1-2B](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/d1-2B)).

## Corrections to the earlier overview

The earlier report was written from search summaries; these notes come from direct reads and overturn several of its statements.

**arXiv 2607.18363 is not a Needle 2 paper.** Its title is "A Controlled Study of Attention-Only Transformers". It never mentions Needle, tool calling or any competitor, and it tests pure attention-only models, not the Hadamard MLP and engram parts that Needle 2 and 3 ship. Its own conclusion is modest: "We claim regime, not superiority" ([arXiv 2607.18363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18363)). All head-to-head tables live in Cactus blog posts, not in a paper.

**Needle 3 is larger, not smaller.** The full model is 121M parameters and 29 MB, against Needle 2's 45M and 14 MB. Only the 4-layer slice is about 8 MB. The "single binary with engine and weights" describes Needle 2 only; Needle 3 ships a separate engine and a weights file ([Cactus, supported devices](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/needle-supported-devices)). The "256-token budget" is Needle 2's sliding attention window, not a hard input limit, and Needle 3's file header gives a context of 8,192 ([Cactus, .cact format post](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/cact-format)). Only Needle v1 is described as distilled from Gemini.

**Mobile Actions is Google's benchmark, not Cactus's**, and the Raspberry Pi article is a Cactus guest post, not an independent test.

**The LEAP SDK is deprecated.** Liquid's docs now say "new projects should use llama.cpp directly", the bundling service is deprecated too, and fine-tuning has moved to the open-source Liquid Finetune stack ([Liquid docs, deprecations](https://docs.liquid.ai/lfm/help/deprecations.md)). The earlier framing of LEAP versus the Cactus SDK as a live competitive seam no longer holds.

**d1 naming.** "Open d1" (7 October 2026) means d1-3B and d1-omni-600M. LFM3 (27 October) shipped d1-2B and d1-Omni-2B. d1.2 and d1.2-ultra (16 November) are hosted API only. d1-3B is already deprecated in favour of d1-2B ([Liquid docs, decision models](https://docs.liquid.ai/lfm/models/decision-models)). The Decision Index is not simply Liquid's own benchmark: the model card links it to a third-party leaderboard, though Liquid ran the scorer itself.

**Cactus is still thanked by Liquid.** The earlier report read Cactus's absence from LFM3 coverage as possible drift; the LFM3 post does thank Cactus in its ecosystem list ([Liquid, LFM3 blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm3)). Liquid has never publicly responded to Needle's benchmark claims.

## Verdict by use case

| Situation | Best choice on current evidence | Why, and the caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Microcontroller, wearable SoC, browser tab or any budget under about 100 MB | Needle 2 or Needle 3, fine-tuned, five tools or fewer | Nothing else fits. Design tools so no arithmetic is needed (a "change by" tool, enums in the user's words) and confirm anything consequential. Accuracy evidence is vendor-only. |
| Phone, Pi-class board or hub with 300 MB or more, your own fixed tool set, willing to fine-tune | FunctionGemma 270M or LFM2.5-350M, fine-tuned | Both exceed 95% on narrow tasks after tuning in partner reports, and FunctionGemma won the only independent tuned head-to-head. Choose by licence: Gemma terms, or LFM's $10M revenue gate. |
| No fine-tuning, varied or changing tools, several calls per request | LFM2.5-230M/350M; Qwen3-0.6B if seconds of latency are acceptable | LFM2.5 leads the untuned small models on BFCL single-turn and on declining; Qwen3-0.6B showed better judgement independently. Expect roughly 60 to 70% exact match, which needs a confirmation step. |
| "Which of N things, and should I act at all?" (routing, approval, guardrails) | A d1 model beside a generator | One pass, calibrated probabilities, no malformed output. Needs about 1 GB or a hosted API; cannot compose arguments. |
| App on a recent iPhone | Apple's bundled model | Zero download and typed output. In Cactus's test it matched the small models on general calling (61.7) but was worst at declining (28.3), and it is Apple-only. |
| Ambiguous, multi-step or high-stakes requests | A 1B+ model or cloud fallback behind a confidence gate | Every sub-1B model fails on implication, negation and chaining; vendors and independent testers agree. |

## Conclusion

The question "which model wins" has a less interesting answer than "which layer does the work". Grammar has solved format. Selection among a few tools is nearly solved by anything, including keyword retrieval. What remains hard is argument values that need a sliver of reasoning and the decision not to act, and those are the two things that shrinking the model damages first. That is why the generative specialist, fine-tuned on the buyer's tools and wrapped in gates, beats both the pure classifier (which cannot write arguments) and the untuned generalist (which is too slow or too large), and why a d1-style gate and a Needle-style generator complement each other. It also explains the direction of travel in Needle 3: each release wraps more hand-written rules around the network, moving it toward a classic intent-and-slot system with a neural core. Size sets the entry ticket and little else. Needle's real, defensible advantage is that it runs where nothing else can. Where 300 MB is available, the evidence does not show that advantage translating into better actions.

Several questions stay open. No neutral party has tested Needle, LFM2.5 and FunctionGemma under one harness, with matched quantization and all models fine-tuned, on both in-domain and out-of-domain tools. Whether Needle's confidence score separates right from wrong calls has no published curve, and the independent anecdotes of wrong calls at confidence 1.0 make this the most important missing measurement. Nobody has published a regex-plus-retrieval-plus-classifier baseline for the tasks where Needle is claimed to shine. Accuracy against the number of tools (5, 20, 50 or more) is unmeasured for every sub-1B model. Whether Mobile Actions-like data shaped Needle 3's jump from 63.7 to 86.0 is not disclosed. No latency or accuracy figure exists for Needle on any microcontroller, no independent energy measurements exist for any of these models, and the only named production deployment (Pebble's ring app) is confirmed solely by Cactus. Finally, the late-2026 state of Apple's and Google's bundled models was not verified from primary sources, so how far operating systems will absorb this niche on phones remains an inference.
