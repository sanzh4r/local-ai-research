# Liquid Builds Models; Cactus Shrinks Them Further

Liquid AI and Cactus Compute both sell "AI that runs on the device instead of the cloud", but they are very different companies that started at different layers of the stack and are now drifting toward each other. Liquid AI is a roughly $2.3B-valued MIT spin-off (about $290M raised) whose product is a family of small, fast, open-weight language and multimodal models called Liquid Foundation Models (LFMs), licensed per device to manufacturers such as Mercedes-Benz and to enterprises such as Shopify. The name comes from "liquid neural networks", a worm-brain-inspired research line, but the commercial models are not those networks: from LFM2 onward they are transformer-like stacks in which most of the expensive attention layers are replaced by cheap, short, gated convolutions, a layout chosen by measuring speed on real phones and laptops. Cactus Compute is an eight-person Y Combinator startup with roughly $1M disclosed funding that began in 2025 as a runtime for running other companies' models (including Liquid's) on phones, and pivoted during 2026 into building its own sub-30 MB task models: Needle for tool calling, Whistle for speech-to-text and Mirage for vision. The two are therefore partners at the runtime layer (Liquid has named Cactus as a deployment route; Cactus natively runs LFM2) and emerging competitors at the tiny-model layer (Cactus benchmarks Needle against Liquid's LFM2.5-230M). One caveat governs everything below: the researchers could not open most web pages directly, so nearly every finding rests on search-result summaries rather than full reads, and nearly every performance number is vendor-reported. The only page read directly was Cactus's GitHub repository.

## A worm with 302 neurons inspired the "liquid" idea

The word "liquid" refers to a 2018-2023 research programme from MIT, TU Wien and ISTA by the people who later founded Liquid AI. It helps to learn three terms in order, because each fixes a problem in the previous one.

**Liquid Time-constant (LTC) networks** came first. An ordinary recurrent neural network updates its memory in fixed ticks with fixed rules, like a metronome. An LTC neuron instead follows a differential equation, and the speed at which it reacts (its "time constant") is itself computed from the incoming signal. The paper describes the models as dynamical systems "with varying (i.e., liquid) time-constants coupled to their hidden state, with outputs being computed by numerical differential equation solvers" ([arXiv 2006.04439](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04439)). A useful analogy is a car's adaptive suspension: a normal shock absorber has one stiffness, while an adaptive one stiffens on a pothole and softens on a smooth road. The neuron's "stiffness" flows with the input, hence "liquid". The cost is the solver: to find out what the neuron does next, the computer must simulate the equation in many tiny time steps, like predicting where a thrown ball lands by nudging it forward a millimetre at a time.

**Closed-form continuous-time (CfC) networks** removed that cost. The authors found a formula that approximates the answer directly, the equivalent of using the physics equation for the ball's landing point instead of simulating the flight. The resulting models are reported as "between one and five orders of magnitude faster in training and inference" than their solver-based counterparts ([arXiv 2106.13898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13898)), and a later comparative study describes CfC as replacing numerical integration "with a single forward pass" at the same accuracy ([arXiv 2510.07578](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.07578)).

**Neural Circuit Policies (NCPs)** are not a new neuron but a wiring diagram. The `ncps` code library describes them as "designed sparse recurrent neural networks loosely inspired by the nervous system of the organism C. elegans" ([ncps repository](https://www.github.com/mlech26l/ncps)), a tiny roundworm. Think of a small company's org chart: sensors report to middle managers, who report to a few decision-makers, who instruct the muscles, with very few lines between them. You can fill that org chart with LTC or CfC neurons. The flagship results were in control, not language: the 2018 precursor paper claimed these policies "perform as good as deep neural network policies with the advantage of realizing interpretable dynamics at the cell level" ([DeepAI mirror](https://deepai.org/publication/re-purposing-compact-neuronal-circuit-policies-to-govern-reinforcement-learning-tasks)), and later papers in Nature Machine Intelligence (2020) and Science Robotics (2023) applied them to driving and drone navigation ([arXiv 2510.07578](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.07578)). The often-quoted details (a 302-neuron worm, a 19-neuron lane-keeping controller) come from the researcher's background knowledge and were **not re-verified** in this research session.

The point to carry forward is that these networks are tiny, continuous-time controllers for sensors and robots. Their strengths (small size, graceful handling of irregular timing, interpretability) have little to do with what makes a language model good, which is scale and parallel training over long text. That gap explains the next section.

## LFMs are mostly short convolutions with a little attention

The commercial Liquid Foundation Models are **not LTC or CfC networks**. They contain no differential equations, no solver, no continuous time and no worm wiring. What survives from the research is one idea, that the operation applied to an input should itself depend on that input, plus the brand and the people. This is the report's reading of the evidence, and it is supported by Liquid's own LFM2 technical report.

The first generation (LFM-1B, LFM-3B and LFM-40B, 30 September 2024) was marketed as "non-transformer" and state of the art, but the weights were never released and the architecture was described only abstractly ([Techmeme](https://www.techmeme.com/240930/p29); [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41698361)). Reception was skeptical: Hacker News users reported confident wrong answers and judged the 3B model roughly on par with Microsoft's Phi 3.5 ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41699459)). Those are anecdotes, but the claims were never independently auditable.

**LFM2 (10 July 2025)** was the first generation with open weights and a technical report, and it is where the real design becomes visible. To understand it, picture how a standard transformer reads. In every layer, every word consults every other word, like a meeting in which each attendee talks to each other attendee; that is "attention", and its cost grows with the square of the prompt length. The model must also keep notes on every past word in every layer (the "KV cache"), so memory grows as the conversation grows. On a data-centre GPU this is tolerable. On a phone CPU it is the bottleneck.

LFM2 keeps the transformer's outer skeleton but swaps the meeting for something cheaper in most layers. Its backbone combines "gated short convolutions" with "a small number of grouped query attention blocks" ([arXiv 2511.23404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404)); the language models interleave **ten convolution blocks with six attention blocks** in a 16-block stack ([Turing Post](https://www.turingpost.com/p/liquidhyena)). A short convolution is a word glancing only at its immediate neighbours (a window of three positions in LFM2-350M), wrapped in learned gates that decide, based on the input, how much to let through ([emergentmind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2511.23404)). In the meeting analogy, ten of sixteen rounds are quick whispers to the people sitting next to you, and only six are all-hands meetings. Local whispers handle grammar and phrasing; the few all-hands rounds remain because only they can fetch a name mentioned five thousand words earlier. The gates are the "liquid" residue: Liquid calls these blocks "Linear Input-Varying" operators, meaning the weights effectively change with the input ([Liquid blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/liquid-foundation-models-v2-our-second-series-of-generative-ai-models)).

How this layout was chosen matters as much as the layout. Liquid ran a **hardware-in-the-loop architecture search**: candidate designs were scored by measured latency and memory on target devices, like a tailor fitting a suit on the customer rather than cutting from a size chart. The search settled on convolutions plus ordinary attention and selected "no additional SSM or linear-attention operators" ([arXiv 2511.23404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404)). That is telling: given a free choice, the search rejected the more exotic machinery. "Hybrid convolution-attention language model" is a more accurate label than "non-transformer", and the edge advantage is an engineering co-design result rather than a consequence of liquid dynamics.

Two later ideas complete the picture. **Mixture-of-experts (MoE)** models such as LFM2-8B-A1B store 8.3B parameters but activate only about 1.5B per token ([Liquid docs](https://docs.liquid.ai/lfm/models/lfm2-8b-a1b)), like a clinic with many specialists where each patient sees only two: storage is cheap on a device, computation is scarce. And **LFM3 (27 October 2026)** adds a middle tier, reported as 13 gated convolutions, 5 sliding-window attention layers with a 256-token window, and 6 global attention layers ([aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/liquid-ai-opens-six-model-lfm3-family-at-256k-context)): neighbours, then a department meeting, then the all-hands. LFM3 details come only from secondary coverage; no LFM3 technical report was found.

The generations at a glance, with dates preferring Liquid's own blog where trackers conflicted:

| Generation | Released | What it is | Openness |
|---|---|---|---|
| LFM1 (1B, 3B, 40B MoE) | 30 Sep 2024 | Text models, architecture undisclosed | Closed weights ([Techmeme](https://www.techmeme.com/240930/p29)) |
| LFM2 (350M to 2.6B dense; 8B and 24B MoE) plus VL, Audio, ColBERT retriever and task "Nanos" | From 10 Jul 2025 | Conv-attention hybrid, 32K context, 10-12T training tokens | Open weights ([arXiv 2511.23404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404)) |
| LFM2.5 (230M to 24B-A2B; Thinking, Japanese, VL, Audio variants) | Jan to Oct 2026 | Same architecture, far more training (28T tokens plus reinforcement learning), up to 128K context | Open weights ([Liquid blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-350m-no-size-left-behind)) |
| LFM3 (six ~2B models from one 1.7B base: text, VL, Audio, Omni, two "d1" decision models) | 27 Oct 2026 | "Tri-hybrid" with sliding-window attention, 256K context | Open weights ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/liquid-ai-launches-lfm3-family-of-six-open-weight-2b-class-models/)) |

"Open weights" is the precise term. The LFM Open License v1.0 allows free commercial use only for organisations under **US$10M annual revenue**; larger companies need a commercial agreement with no public price ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-liquid-ai-opens-lfm3-free-to-firms-under-10m-in-revenue/)). Rivals such as Qwen, Phi and SmolLM ship under Apache or MIT terms ([Turing Post](https://www.turingpost.com/p/slmslist)).

On performance, Liquid's consistent claim is "the quality of the next size up, at about twice the CPU speed": the technical report states "up to 2x faster prefill and decode on CPUs compared to similarly sized models" ([arXiv 2511.23404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404)), and LFM2.5-1.2B-Thinking is said to fit in about 900 MB on a phone ([Liquid blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-1-2b-thinking-on-device-reasoning-under-1gb)). **These are company-run numbers.** Coverage of LFM3 notes that all figures are self-reported with no independent evaluations ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329588/20261028/liquid-ai-releases-lfm3-six-open-weight-models-fuse-text-vision-audio-one-2b-backbone.htm)), and no controlled third-party replication of the 2x claim was found. The little independent evidence is mixed: a distil labs fine-tuning study found LFM2-1.2B beating several larger models ([distil labs](https://www.distillabs.ai/learn/qwen3-1-7b-vs-gemma-3-1b-for-classification/)), while a Hacker News user reported "serious coherence issues" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47957648)). Liquid itself does not recommend LFM2.5-2.6B for agentic coding or knowledge-heavy tasks ([Liquid blog](https://liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-2-6b)). The pattern is coherent with the design: models whose layers mostly see three-word neighbourhoods and that have few parameters are good at following formats, calling tools and extracting data, and weak at stored knowledge and long reasoning. Note also that the LFM2 to LFM2.5 quality jump came from training, not architecture, so the architecture's defensible edge is speed and memory per parameter rather than intelligence per parameter.

## Liquid sells per-device licences, Arm-style

Liquid AI was founded in March 2023 as an MIT CSAIL spin-off by Ramin Hasani (CEO), Mathias Lechner (CTO), Alexander Amini and Daniela Rus, the authors of the liquid-network papers ([Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/mit-spinoff-unveils-new-generation-of-ai-models-with-liquid-neural-network-technology/); [Boston Globe](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/boston-sunday-globe/20231210/281968907467589)). It raised a $37.5M seed and then a **$250M Series A led by AMD in December 2024** at about $2.35B ([CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/liquid-ai-secures-250m-in-massachusetts-largest-fundraise-of-the-year); [Salestools](https://salestools.io/en/report/liquid-ai-250m-series-a)). Funding databases disagree on the total ($287.5M to $297M), and no later priced round is confirmed: a $4.5B valuation cited in one Substack essay and a "Series B" label on one review site are **unverified**. Headcount is undisclosed, with third-party estimates from 50 to 130.

The business model resembles a chip-design licensor more than a cloud API. In an October 2026 interview, Hasani said Liquid makes money primarily by licensing models and software to manufacturers, generally as an annual per-device fee on top of development costs, ranging from under $1 to hundreds of dollars per device ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/stocks/liquid-ai-ceo-says-ai-next-big-opportunity-inside-car-assistant)). The open weights work as a funnel: small developers adopt freely, and anyone above $10M revenue must negotiate. A hosted API arrived only in October 2026; one reviewer says Liquid previously "sold no tokens at all" ([IADecider](https://iadecider.com/articles/liquid-ai-review)). Around the models sits LEAP, the Liquid Edge AI Platform, an SDK, model library, fine-tuning scripts and bundling tool for iOS, Android and laptops ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250715701268/en/Liquid-AI-Launches-LEAP-and-Apollo-A-New-Era-for-Edge-AI-Deployment-Begins); [LEAP platform](https://leap.liquid.ai/platform)). Profiles describe LEAP as deploying LFMs across third-party engines such as llama.cpp, MLX and ONNX ([hokai.io](https://hokai.io/hub/companies/liquid-ai)): Liquid does not build its own inference engine.

Named relationships show where the money is aimed. **Mercedes-Benz** announced a multi-year partnership in April 2026 to run LFMs inside MBUX-equipped cars in North America for speech, language understanding and reasoning ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260423009970/en/Mercedes-Benz-and-Liquid-AI-Partner-to-Scale-Embedded-In-Car-Intelligence-in-North-America)); a reported over-the-air rollout starting October 2026 is known only through an aggregator citing the Wall Street Journal. **Shopify**, also an investor, licenses LFMs for sub-20-millisecond search ([Liquid press](https://www.liquid.ai/press/liquid-ai-announces-multi-year-partnership-with-shopify-to-bring-sub-20ms-foundation-models-to-core-commerce-experiences)), which shows that "edge" for Liquid includes latency-critical servers. Others include G42, Insilico Medicine ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/insilico-medicine-and-liquid-ai-announce-strategic-partnership-delivering-lightweight-scientific-foundation-models-for-drug-discovery)) and MacPaw ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/macpaw-partners-liquid-ai-bring-120000537.html)). No financial terms are verified for any deal, and the only revenue figure (about $13.2M for 2025) is a database estimate, not a disclosure ([GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/liquid.ai)). The product line is also widening beyond chat: "d1" decision models that return a typed decision in a single pass with no generated text ([Hugging Face blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/LiquidAI/open-d1)) and LiquidCodec, a roughly 74M-parameter speech tokenizer ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/12/liquid-ai-liquidcodec-streaming-audio-codec/)).

## Cactus pivoted from engine to 14 MB models

Cactus Compute was founded in 2025 by Roman Shemet (CEO) and Henry Ndubuaku (CTO), went through Y Combinator's Summer 2025 batch, and is listed with 8 employees in San Francisco ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/cactus)). Directories put disclosed funding at **$0.5M to $1M** ([EveryDev](https://www.everydev.ai/developers/cactus-compute); [Extruct](https://www.extruct.ai/hub/cactuscompute-com/)); no primary announcement of a priced round was found, and databases often confuse it with an unrelated YC company called Cactus (oncactus.com) that raised $7M.

A kitchen analogy clarifies the stack and Cactus's move. Silicon is the stove; the inference runtime is the chef who actually cooks; the model is the recipe; quantization is shrinking the recipe card by rounding the quantities; the SDK is the waiter who brings the dish to an app. Liquid writes recipes. **Cactus started as a chef.** Its September 2025 launch offered an engine for running any Hugging Face language or vision model on smartphones, with React Native, Flutter and Kotlin bindings ([Launch HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45291024)). Its v1 rewrite had three layers: hand-written ARM kernels, a computation graph described as "PyTorch-like for mobile", and an engine with OpenAI-compatible APIs and optional cloud handoff ([Cactus docs](https://docs.cactuscompute.com/v1.12/)). It ran third-party models converted to Cactus's own format, including Gemma, Qwen and Liquid's LFM2 ([Cactus docs](https://docs.cactuscompute.com/v1.11/)).

During 2026 Cactus began writing its own recipes and selling them pre-cooked. Its GitHub repository, read directly on 16 November 2026, now leads with "Sub-50mb agentic, voice and vision intelligence for consumer devices" and states that "the earlier general inference SDK is now `cactus-engine`" ([GitHub](https://github.com/cactus-compute/cactus)). The YC tagline changed from a "low-latency AI engine" to "Automation Foundation Models For Tiny Devices". The models are:

| Model | Released | Job | Size and claims (company-reported unless noted) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Needle v1 | May 2026 | Tool calling | 26M parameters, 14 MB, distilled from Gemini ([NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/cactus-releases-needle-a-26m-distilled-model-for-on-device-tool-calling/)) |
| Needle 2 | Aug 2026 | Tool calling | 45M parameters, one 14 MB binary with engine and 2-bit weights, 28 MB RAM ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/13/cactus-compute-needle-2-45m-parameter-tool-calling-model/)) |
| Needle 3 | 17 Sep 2026 | Tool calling, extraction, embeddings | 8-29 MB; 86.0% on Cactus's own Mobile Actions benchmark ([Better Stack](https://betterstack.com/community/guides/ai/needle3-edge/)) |
| Whistle | 2 Oct 2026 | Speech-to-text | 16.9 MB, CPU-only, seven languages ([RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/cactus-whistle-16-9mb-local-speech-model)) |
| Mirage | Nov 2026 | Vision | 22 MB; detection, OCR, extraction ([GitHub](https://github.com/cactus-compute/cactus/blob/main/README.md)) |

Needle is best understood as a switchboard operator, not a conversationalist. It takes an instruction plus a list of available tools and emits one structured function call; a third-party reviewer notes it has no free-text fallback and a hard 256-token budget ([Mi&Bee blog](https://blog.mickeyzzc.tech/en/posts/aihelper/needle2-on-device-tool-calling/)). Liquid's smallest model is hundreds of megabytes; Cactus's are tens. That is the difference between a pocket phrasebook and a single laminated card of emergency phrases. Cactus also has a quantization line, "Cactus Quants", which it applied to a 120B Nvidia model at a claimed 1.418 bits per weight; its own follow-up concedes heavy quality loss, with the compressed model solving 7 tasks against the original's 24 ([Cactus blog](https://cactuscompute.com/blog/quantization-and-multi-step-workflows)).

Independent checks on Cactus are few but slightly better than for Liquid. Raspberry Pi's own news blog ran Needle on a Pi 5 and saw a command turned into an action in 78 ms ([Raspberry Pi](https://www.raspberrypi.com/news/turn-text-input-into-actions-with-needle-a-14mb-function-calling-llm/)). An independent critique of Whistle found it beats Whisper base on two of six comparable benchmarks, "not most", and that the marketing comparison mixed decoding settings ([ai.satyajit.si](https://ai.satyajit.si/articles/whistle-stt)). The models run fast on small hardware; the comparisons are selective.

Monetization is the weakest-evidenced part. Cactus moved from Apache 2.0 to a non-commercial licence just before its 2025 launch, drawing criticism, with paid licences priced per monthly active device ([Launch HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45291024)); later terms were reported as free below $2M in funding and revenue ([ai-tldr](https://ai-tldr.dev/tools/cactus/)). As of November 2026 the main repository is back under Apache 2.0, including the runtime binaries ([GitHub](https://github.com/cactus-compute/cactus/blob/main/README.md)), and no public pricing exists. The licence of the legacy `cactus-engine` is unconfirmed. **How Cactus currently earns revenue is unknown**; named production users (AnythingLLM, KinAI) date from September 2025 and are company claims. Traction is community-led: 14.8k GitHub stars.

## Partners at the runtime, rivals at the tiny end

| | Liquid AI | Cactus Compute |
|---|---|---|
| Core product | LFM model families (230M to 24B), plus LEAP SDK and hosted API | Sub-30 MB task models (Needle, Whistle, Mirage) on its own C++ engine; legacy general engine; quantization format |
| Models it builds | General-purpose small language, vision, audio, omni and decision models | Narrow single-task models; no general chat |
| Stack layer | Model layer, reaching up into tooling; relies on third-party runtimes | Began at runtime/SDK; now model plus runtime bundled together |
| Target hardware | Phones, PCs, cars, Raspberry Pi-class boards, on-prem servers | Wearables, smart home, small robots, budget phones, Raspberry Pi |
| Business model | Per-device licences and custom work for OEMs and enterprises; weights free under $10M revenue | Unclear in 2026; earlier per-active-device licence and paid cloud fallback; now Apache 2.0 |
| Scale | ~$290M raised, ~$2.35B valuation (Dec 2024), named enterprise customers | ~8 people, ~$1M disclosed, no recent named customers |
| Evidence quality | Vendor benchmarks; a technical report; mixed anecdotes | Vendor benchmarks; a few independent hands-on tests |

The relationship is a non-exclusive ecosystem tie, not a formal alliance. On the partner side, Liquid's LFM2-24B-A2B launch post lists Cactus among routes for deploying "across mobile, desktop, and terminal", alongside Ollama, LM Studio and Nexa AI ([Liquid blog](https://www.liquid.ai/en/blog/lfm2-24b-a2b-from-cloud-to-ai-pc)); Liquid's LFM2.5-350M materials cite Cactus demonstrations on sub-$300 devices ([Liquid blog](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-350m-no-size-left-behind)); and Cactus hosts converted LFM weights and says its role is complementary: "Cactus provides the runtime, while Liquid AI provides the models" ([Cactus](https://cactuscompute.com/compare/cactus-vs-liquid-ai)). No press release, investment or commercial agreement between them was found.

On the competitor side, three seams have opened. Cactus benchmarks Needle 2 against Liquid's LFM2.5-230M and Google's FunctionGemma ([RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/cactus-needle-2-14mb-agent-model-tiny-devices)), so in tool calling and extraction the two now sell substitutes. Liquid's LEAP and Cactus's SDK both answer the developer question "how do I ship a small model in my app". And Cactus publishes "best Liquid AI alternative" pages that describe Liquid as needing a third-party runtime for mobile, a framing that understates LEAP. A possible sign of drift: Cactus was not among the partners surfaced at the LFM3 launch, which named silicon vendors and distribution platforms ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/liquid-ai-launches-lfm3-family-of-six-open-weight-2b-class-models/)), and whether Cactus supports LFM3 is unknown. This absence could simply reflect what search snippets surfaced.

Both face the same larger pressure. Google, Microsoft, Alibaba and Hugging Face give small models away under permissive licences, and operating-system vendors are absorbing the layer: Apple introduced Core AI at WWDC 2026 as its framework for running models on-device ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/382/?time=1271)). An OS-bundled model costs a developer no download and no licence. Market-size estimates for on-device AI vary more than fivefold by definition, from about $11B in 2025 ([Spherical Insights](https://www.sphericalinsights.com/blogs/top-10-companies-in-global-on-device-ai-market-2026-2035-spherical-insights-analysis)) to $66B in 2026 for broader "on-device intelligence" ([Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6243836/on-device-intelligence-market-outlook-market)); these come from paid-report vendors with unverified methods, mostly include hardware, and none sizes the model-licensing or SDK slice.

## How much of this to trust

The research notes carry four limitations that a reader should hold in mind. First, **access**: direct fetches of liquid.ai, arxiv.org, huggingface.co, cactuscompute.com, ycombinator.com and news sites were blocked, so all findings except the Cactus GitHub page come from search-engine summaries of the cited pages. Quotes are as surfaced and the full pages were not read. Second, **vendor reporting**: almost every speed, size and benchmark figure for both companies originates with the company, often on self-designed benchmarks (Liquid's internal suites and "Decision Index"; Cactus's "Mobile Actions"), and suites change between releases. Third, **recency**: several late-2026 items (LFM3 internals, d1.2, Mirage, the Mercedes rollout) rest on secondary coverage or a title alone. Fourth, **internal conflict**: the landscape notes describe Cactus as a runtime company, whereas the directly read repository shows the model pivot; this report follows the direct read.

Specific items to treat as unverified: Liquid's $4.5B valuation, any Series B and the August 2026 PitchBook deal; Liquid's revenue and headcount; the exact LTC and CfC equations and the 19-neuron and 302-neuron figures; the claim that LFM2 was distilled from an internal LFM1 teacher; ExecuTorch support in LEAP today; exact release dates where trackers disagree with Liquid's blog; Cactus's funding amount and backers (Oxford Seed Fund, Google for Startups); Cactus's non-Apple NPU support; the licence of `cactus-engine`; Cactus's claimed 3,000 developers; a statement attributed to Liquid recommending "a mobile inference engine like Cactus"; and any Cactus deal with phone makers, for which a targeted search found nothing.

## Conclusion

The cleanest way to hold both companies in mind is that they are approaching the same destination from opposite ends. Liquid started with a scientific story and a general-purpose model, then moved downward in size (230M parameters) and outward into non-chat decision models. Cactus started with plumbing, found that plumbing alone was hard to monetize against free runtimes, and moved upward into models so small that model and engine ship as one file. They meet in "tiny, task-specific, always-on" AI: turning a spoken or typed instruction into an action on a cheap device. The partnership at the runtime layer is real but shallow; the overlap in tool-calling models is recent and growing. A second insight is that neither company's headline technology is its moat. Liquid's "liquid" science is mostly heritage, and its advantage is hardware-measured efficiency plus OEM contracts; Cactus's kernels are now a supporting asset, and its advantage is extreme smallness under a permissive licence. Whether either advantage survives free big-tech models and OS-bundled AI is the open question.
